Kitchen renovation costs — $14,000 to $80,000

The kitchen is consistently the most expensive room to renovate and the one with the highest ROI. The wide cost range reflects enormous variation in cabinet quality — stock cabinets cost $3,000–$8,000 while custom cabinets cost $15,000–$40,000 for the same kitchen.

Bathroom renovation costs — $6,000 to $50,000

Bathroom renovation cost depends heavily on bathroom type and whether plumbing is moved. A half-bath refresh costs $2,500–$8,000. A full bath renovation runs $7,000–$20,000. A master bath transformation can reach $50,000 with premium materials and custom tile work.

The #1 cost-saving rule in bathrooms: keep plumbing fixtures in place. Moving a toilet or shower drain requires cutting through concrete, adding $2,000–$5,000 to your project immediately.

Basement finishing costs — $15,000 to $70,000

Finishing a basement is the most efficient way to add livable square footage because the structure already exists. Cost runs $20–$80 per square foot depending on finish level. A basic finish (open layout, carpet, basic lighting) for a 1,000 sq ft basement costs $20,000–$30,000. A premium finish with a full bathroom and wet bar costs $45,000–$70,000 for the same space.

Basement finishing returns 65–75% at resale and adds daily quality of life immediately. It is one of the highest-value renovations available for most homeowners.

Roof replacement costs — $4,000 to $75,000

Roof replacement is priced per square (100 sq ft) not per room. A standard 1,500 sq ft home has a roof of 1,800–2,200 sq ft accounting for pitch and overhang. Asphalt shingles ($3–$5/sq ft) are the most common and affordable option. Metal roofing ($7–$14/sq ft) costs more upfront but lasts 40–70 years. Premium tile and slate ($15–$40/sq ft) are for high-end homes with the structural capacity to support the weight.

3 quality levels — what each means

Budget quality

Contractor-grade materials available at big-box home improvement stores. Builder-grade cabinets, laminate countertops, basic tile, stock lighting fixtures. Appropriate for rental properties, flips, or resale-focused renovations where ROI matters more than personal preferences.

Standard quality

Mid-range materials from established brands. Semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite countertops, name-brand fixtures, hardwood or quality LVP flooring. The most popular choice for owner-occupied renovations. Balances quality, durability, and cost effectively.

Premium quality

High-end materials and custom work. Full custom cabinets, natural stone countertops, designer fixtures, solid hardwood flooring, custom tile work. Appropriate for luxury homes, homeowners planning to stay long-term, or projects in markets where premium finishes are expected.

What to do with your estimate

Your renovation cost estimate is a starting point — a market-rate benchmark for your specific room, size, state, and quality level. Here is how to use it effectively:

  1. Compare it to your available budget to determine if the project is feasible at your desired quality level
  2. Use it to decide between full renovation, partial renovation, and cosmetic refresh
  3. Present it to contractors: "I've researched the market rate for this project — can you explain how your quote compares?"
  4. Add 15% contingency to account for unexpected discoveries during construction
  5. Get at least 3 written quotes and compare each against the estimate

Frequently asked questions

Which room has the best renovation ROI?

For dollar-for-dollar ROI, a minor kitchen refresh consistently ranks first — returning 75–85% of cost at resale in most markets. For total value added per project, finishing a basement returns the most absolute dollars because you are converting raw space into livable area at low cost per sq ft.

How do I measure my room for a renovation estimate?

For standard rooms (kitchen, bathroom, bedroom, living room), measure the floor area: length × width in feet. For roofs, enter the total roof area — your attic floor area plus about 20% for pitch typically gives a good estimate. If you are unsure, most estimates using the midpoint of typical room sizes for your home age and type are accurate within 15%.

What is included in a renovation cost estimate?

Our estimates include labor, materials, fixtures, and permits for the selected room type at the chosen quality level. They do not include design fees, furniture, temporary housing costs, or landscaping repair. Add 5–10% for these ancillary costs on complex projects.

How often are renovation costs updated?

We update our cost database annually based on contractor survey data, materials price indices, and BLS labor data. Our current data reflects 2026 market conditions including recent increases in materials and labor costs.
